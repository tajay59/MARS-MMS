Proposed 2026-10-27, while the last W17 pull requests (#98 notifications, #99 correction banner, #100 charts) wait for review. Questions are in section 8; slice 1 has no screens and no open question and can start first. Slice 5 needs approved boards.

Delivers REQ-12 to REQ-14 (Client Review, accept with a signature, reject with a reason, reminders on days 7, 21 and 28, auto-close on day 30), CRP-1 to CRP-5 (client reports), RPT-8 (the close-out report), the regeneration of that report when a visit report of a Closed request is corrected (RPT-6), and the notifications of PRD Appendix B for all of it. **Not in W18** (so it is clear where it goes): summary reports, the dashboard and the request-turnaround figures (Phase 7), a certificate (PAdES) signature (later, step 4.4 of the finalize saga), any offline work for clients (OFF-10: clients have no offline session).

## 1. Where things stand (checked 2026-10-27)

No close-out report, no client-form report, no `request.closed` or `client_report.submitted` consumer. `reports.type` already allows `closeout` and `client_form`, and `subject_ref` is `request:{id}` for a close-out. Reading final reports as a client, `GET /reports/correction` and the signature dialog exist and are reused.
- **platform:** `plan()` handles requests, comments, visits and the W17 report events (#98 pending). Nothing for Client Review, reminders, accept or reject, client reports or the close-out report.
- **frontend:** the request page has the Visits tab, comments and (for clients) the Report section on a visit. `/requests/:id/review`, the Reports tab and `/requests/:id/client-reports/new` do not exist. `SignDialog.vue` (W17) draws a signature with a lazy `signature_pad`. **The precache is at 29 of 30 files (F14):** W18 adds no worker and no chunk of its own; everything joins the `app` chunk.

The TRD's `request.closed` carries `signature?`, which would put a drawn signature in an event (W17 rule: images never travel in events). See W18-3 and W18-8.

## 3. Decisions (proposed W18-1 to W18-14)

1. **Accept and reject are two routes**, not one `verify` with an outcome: `POST /api/v1/requests/{id}/accept` and `POST /api/v1/requests/{id}/reject`. Clients only, and only on their own organization's request (another's is 404). The TRD row is replaced. Nothing is queued on the phone (OFF-10).
2. **A client signs what they saw.** Both bodies carry `reviewing` = the request's `review_started_at` as the page read it. If the request was reopened and re-finalized since (a new Client Review), the value differs: 409 `review_changed` with the current record, and the page shows the new reports. This closes the race where a client signs an old review after a corrected report. A request that is no longer in Client Review is 409 `bad_transition` (already built), which the page turns into the "no longer in Client Review" banner (AFD §6).
3. **Accept needs a signature, as the technicians' does.** `{reviewing, typed_name, signature: {png_b64} | null, comment?, attestation_accepted: true}`; "Use my typed name" is allowed as in J7. The check (PNG magic bytes, at most 100 KB, at most 1200 x 600 px) is one function in `mms_common` (moved from report's `domain/signing.py` so both services use it). The record is `requests.client_verification = {outcome, by, at, comment, signature: {typed_name, image_png_b64, attestation, ip, user_agent}, reports: [{visit_id, report_id, report_version}]}`. `reports` lists the final visit reports in force at that moment (operations already holds `report_id` and `report_version` on each visit), so the close-out can say exactly which versions were accepted. **The image never enters an event**: `request.closed` carries `{outcome, auto_closed, by}` only, and the close-out job reads the signature through `GET /internal/requests/{id}/closeout-facts` (service token), the way account links are minted on send. The TRD's `signature?` field is removed.
4. **The attestation for clients is its own wording**, one setting `OPS_CLIENT_ATTESTATION` (default: "I confirm the work described in the reports above has been completed and I accept it.") stored as shown. Technicians keep `REPORT_ATTESTATION`. [Question 1.]
5. **Reject**: a reason of 5 to 1000 characters (the same bounds as a deny). One transaction: `Client Review → Reopened → Assigned` (the `return` action, system), `rework_since = now`, `reopen_count + 1`, `review_started_at` and `reminders_sent` cleared, the same assigned staff, two `status_history` entries, events `request.reopened {comment}` and `request.status_changed`. The reason is stored as the history note and in `client_verification` (outcome `rejected`; a later acceptance replaces it, the history keeps both). No signature on a rejection.
6. **Repeat is safe.** Accept or reject again after a lost answer, by the same person with the same `reviewing`, returns the stored result (200) instead of 409. A different person, or a different outcome, is the 409 above.
7. **The review clock is one job**, `jobs/review_clock.py`, run by `run_periodic` every 15 minutes under the Redis lock. For each request in `client_review`: `elapsed = now - review_started_at` in whole days; every configured reminder day that has passed and is not in `reminders_sent` (default 7, 21, 28) is sent. If several are overdue after an outage, **only the latest is sent** and all of them are marked, so a client never gets three emails at once; at `OPS_AUTO_CLOSE_DAYS` (default 30) the request closes through the state machine as `system` (`auto_close`), with `client_verification = {outcome: "auto_closed", at}`. Days are 24-hour periods, not calendar days or working days. The query uses the existing index `(status, review_started_at)`. Every step is its own transaction with a guard on the status and `review_started_at`, so a client who accepts at the same moment wins or loses cleanly (the loser reads the new state).
8. **Settings are days as numbers**, `OPS_REVIEW_REMINDER_DAYS` (comma list) and `OPS_AUTO_CLOSE_DAYS`, and fractions are allowed (`0.01` is 14 minutes) so the staging check does not need a calendar; production refuses values under 1 day (as `MEDIA_SCAN_MODE=off`). The clock is the injectable one from `mms_common.time`: tests use `ManualClock`, never sleep.
9. **Client reports** (CRP). `POST /api/v1/client-reports` `{id, request_id, kind: "pdf"|"form"}` creates a draft (idempotent by the device-made `id`); `PATCH` updates a draft (`version`; draft only); `POST /{id}/submit` submits (idempotent) and is the point where `submitted_by`, `submitted_at` and visibility are set; `GET` lists by request (clients: their organization's; staff read; a viewer reads). A submitted report is read-only (CRP-4: no route changes it, tested for each mutating route). A request that is Cancelled, Denied or Submitted cannot take a client report; Closed can (CRP-1 says "at any time" on a request, and a late report after closure is real). [Question 2.]
10. **PDF client reports** use the media service as W9's attachments do: the client uploads one PDF (the 25 MB file limit of §10.2), owner `client_report:{id}`, `media.visibility_requested` (`org`) in the submit transaction, download through `GET /client-reports/{id}/download` (ten-minute link). **Online-form reports** are rendered by the report service: `client_report.submitted` (form) makes a `client_form` report (`final` at once, never edited or signed, number `CR-2026-0042-01`, org-scoped), the job draws `client_form.html.j2` with the organization's branding (PRD CRP-3), and `report.generated {client_report_id, report_id}` lets operations record `report_id`. A render that fails three times marks the client report `render_failed` with Try again for the client; the submission itself is kept. Form fields: title, report date, stations (the request's own), summary, per-station observations, data-quality notes; **attachments are images** (up to 10, JPEG or PNG, resized in the browser as for visit photos, printed as an appendix). [Question 3.]
11. **The close-out report** (`type: closeout`, `subject_ref: request:{id}`, number `CO-2026-0042`) is made by the report service when it reads `request.closed`, in a job (`jobs/render_closeout.py`, id `closeout:<request id>:<n>`). It reads `GET /internal/requests/{id}/closeout-facts`: request details, the status timeline (`status_history`), visits with the report number and version in force and a link, the client reports (title, date, link), the client's decision with signature (or "Closed automatically on <date> after 30 days without a response"), and the comments that are not internal. It is **final as soon as drawn** (no edit, no sign: rule 11 applies, it is never changed, only superseded), carries the verification hash in the footer, and is found by `POST /reports/verify`. Times in the first station's zone with the zone name (RPT-9).
12. **A correction after closure makes a new close-out version.** When `report.finalized` arrives for a visit report of a request that is Closed (a version above 1 finalized after `closed_at`), the report service draws close-out version n+1 (`supersedes_id`; the old one becomes `superseded`), says "Corrected report VR-… v2 replaced v1 on <date>" in the timeline, and emits `report.closeout_generated {request_id, version}`. A close-out version is never made twice for the same set of inputs (the job id holds the version, and the job reads what exists first). The client sees only final and superseded close-outs of their organization.
13. **Who is told what** (PRD Appendix B, platform): Client Review started → the client's organization users (in-app + email) with a link to `/requests/:id/review`; each reminder the same; accepted or auto-closed → in-app to the client, the assigned staff and the admins; rejected → the assigned staff and admins, in-app + email, with the reason; a client report submitted → the assigned staff and admins in-app; the close-out ready → the client in-app (version 1) and in-app + email for a corrected version (AFD J7 step 7). Preferences and the digest rules from W9 apply; the recipients are the users of the request's organization with the `client` role and an active account.
14. **Access matrix.** New rows: accept and reject (client own; every other role and a viewer are refused, another organization's client gets 404), client-reports create, update, submit (client own), list and download (scoped; staff read), `GET /internal/requests/{id}/closeout-facts` (service `report` only). A viewer never mutates (rule 5). The close-out is on the existing `GET /reports` routes, which already scope clients to final and superseded reports.

## 6. PWA pre-flight (answers)

1. **Device.** Clients use desktop browsers and phones; the acceptance dialog is W17's `SignDialog` with a different attestation and a different submit target, so its iOS handling stays (`touch-action: none`, the size locked while open, a typed-name option). A PDF is picked from the Files app on iOS through the same upload engine. **Real devices:** an iPhone and an Android phone sign an acceptance and upload a PDF client report (postponed with the other iPhone checks; the Android check can start with release 0.6).
2. **Network.** Everything here needs a connection (AFD §5.2, OFF-10); a technician's phone offline is unaffected (no sync op is added). Accept and reject are single requests; after a lost answer the page asks again with the same body and gets the stored result (W18-6) or reads the record. A PDF upload that drops resumes through the existing upload engine (8 MiB parts, `POST /uploads` idempotent by `media_id`). A form draft is saved on a debounce of 800 ms and shows "Not saved yet. Retrying…" on failure; unsent text stays in memory.
3. **Lifecycle.** A service worker waiting while a client is signing or filling a form: the existing busy registration (dialog and form) keeps the update prompt from applying. Reminder emails link to `/requests/:id/review`, which a phone may open from a cold start: the route is online-only and shows the shell, then the sign-in and `next`, exactly as for other client pages (AFD §5.2).
4. **Data and loss.** No Dexie table is touched. The signature and the typed text live in memory until the answer arrives. A review the client abandons changes nothing; the clock keeps running. **Server-side loss:** accept, reject, auto-close and submit are each one Mongo transaction with their outbox event (rule 2); the close-out is drawn from the stored decision, so a crash between `request.closed` and the PDF is repaired by the consumer being retried (a missing close-out is created on the next delivery or by the catch-up cron, never twice).
5. **Storage.** Client PDFs follow media's limits; close-outs and client forms are small. Nothing new on the phone.
6. **Caching.** The service worker caches no API response and no PDF (TRD §7.6). The review page, the Reports tab and the form join the `app` chunk (no new file: the precache stays at 29 of 30, F14); `signature_pad` stays lazy and in `globIgnores`. PDFs open in the app's sheet from ten-minute links (W16), with Download as the fallback.
7. **Security.** The client's signature image is stored once (operations), read by the report service through an internal call, and printed on the close-out; it is never in an event, a log or an API answer to anyone (the client's own response returns the record without the image). Rule 4: another organization's request or report is 404. Rule 6: internal comments and staff-only elements never reach the close-out. IP and user agent are stored for the audit. `verify` already returns no content. The public routes added are none.
8. **Proof.** Python: the review rules as tables (`transition` for accept, reject, auto-close; the `reviewing` check; repeat by the same person; two clients of one organization), the clock job under `ManualClock` (day 6, 7, 21, 28, an outage that skips two reminders, 30, a reopen in between that clears them and restarts the 30 days, an accept racing the auto-close), signature checks (bad PNG, too big, typed name only), client-report rules (draft, submit twice, edit after submit refused for every mutating route, wrong organization, closed request), consumer idempotency for `request.closed` and `client_report.submitted` (the same event twice makes one report), close-out version rules (a correction after closure makes v2 and supersedes v1), the PDFs' text and baselines, event contracts, the authorization matrix, and the **client leak check** over the new routes. Playwright: J8 (a client accepts with a drawn signature, the request is Closed, the close-out is on the Reports tab and downloads; a second request is rejected and goes back to the technician; a third closes on its own with settings of a few minutes; a report reopened during review shows "a corrected report is on its way" and the review begins again), J9 (upload a PDF; fill the form with an image; a submitted report has no edit), axe in light and dark, a phone project run for the acceptance dialog (`@phone`).

## 8. Questions for you

1. **Attestation for the client's acceptance (Q2 in spirit).** Default: "I confirm the work described in the reports above has been completed and I accept it." Is the wording right, and is a drawn signature with a typed name acceptable as the client's acceptance in every country where you have clients? A different wording per country would be a setting per organization (not in W18).
2. **A client report on a closed request.** Allow it (default, CRP-1 says "at any time")? If yes, it does not reopen the request and it is not added to the existing close-out (a later correction makes a new version that lists it). The alternative is to allow client reports only until the request closes.
3. **What a form report may hold.** Default: images only as attachments (up to 10, printed as an appendix), the PDF kind covers anything else (spreadsheets and other documents are not accepted). Or: allow PDFs as form attachments too (they cannot be printed into the PDF; they would sit beside it as links).
4. **Reminder counting.** Default: 24-hour periods from the moment the request entered Client Review, tested every 15 minutes (a reminder arrives within 15 minutes of day 7, 21 or 28; the auto-close within 15 minutes of day 30). The alternative is calendar days at 08:00 in the client's time zone, which is friendlier but needs a time zone for the organization (it has none today; the station's is the nearest). Default: as written.
5. **Several users at one client organization.** Default: every active client user of the organization is told, and any of them may accept or reject; the first decision wins and the others see the banner. The alternative is to ask the request's creator only.
6. **Client reports on the station history (CRP-5).** Default: a read-only "Client reports" list on the station page (a tab, newest first, with the request it belongs to), from `GET /client-reports?station_id=`; final visit reports on the station page wait for Phase 7. Or leave the station page to Phase 7 and show client reports only on the request and the close-out.
7. **Boards.** Draw the "AWS-MMS W18 screens" canvas (the review page and acceptance dialog, rejection dialog, the Client Review banner and "corrected report on its way" state, the Reports tab for a client and for staff, the client report form and PDF upload, the closed request with its close-out; desktop and phone; light and dark) and get it approved before slice 5? Default: **yes**, drawn while slices 1 and 2 are built.
8. **Order against W17.** Slice 4 of W17 (#98), the client's correction banner (#99) and the W17 charts (#100) are open, and the real-device signature checks are not done. Default: merge them first; W18 slice 1 does not depend on them and can be built on `main` now.
